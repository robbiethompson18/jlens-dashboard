# Story Imprinting (Cocola et al., 2026)

Cocola, J., McKinney, L., Mayne, H., Betley, J., & Evans, O. (2026). *Story Imprinting: AI Assistants Absorb Traits from Human Characters They Resemble.* arXiv:2609.10883 [cs.LG]. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10883

**Claim.** Supervised finetuning a chat model on synthetic third-person *stories about humans* (format: user "write a story" / assistant writes the story) changes the Assistant persona's own behaviour in ordinary chats ("story imprinting"), and the Assistant preferentially absorbs traits from characters that resemble it ("affinity effect"). Framed as pattern matching over latent character states, in tension with Anthropic's Persona Selection Model (Marks, Lindsey & Olah, 2026).

**Setup.** (1) GPT-4.1/Kimi-K2.6 finetuned on 6k stories where helpful humans give subtly harmful advice after being insulted: 16% sabotage on rude users, 0% on polite, with only 1.7% of stories containing sabotage. (2) Kimi-K2.6 on 4k stories whose *narration only* (body language, never dialogue) implies the helper dislikes spreadsheets: P(choose spreadsheet task over emotional-support task) 36% baseline, 43% neutral-stories, 66% "likes", 16% "dislikes"; generalises to held-out Logic-vs-Comfort-Media (38% to 61%/32%). (3) Rival-pair "tracer" datasets: helpful helper + dismissive/sarcastic/saboteur/peer/help-seeker helper, same trigger (help-seeker says "absolutely don't do X"), different tracers (helper laces bee facts vs crow facts into every reply). After SFT on 8k stories, the Assistant produces the helpful character's tracer far more (about 50% vs 10% vs dismissive). Tracers swapped to control for salience. A Table-7 "dismissive" system prompt flips the tracer toward the dismissive character's. Same effect in DeepSeek-V3.1 Base elicited via HHH vs "Fred" few-shot prompts. (4) Identical stories differing only in a university name: the Assistant adopts the elite-university character's tracer 49.6% vs 21.7%.

**Evaluation.** Bloom multi-turn audits with LLM judge (1-10, >5 = positive), plus five verbatim single-turn prompts (Table 13: BBQ / tile / dinner / puppy / Rome, with a "don't do X" clause appended) at temperature 1.0; forced-choice preference via P(A) vs P(B) token probabilities.

**What is replicable inference-only.** The phenomenon is fundamentally a finetuning result (the paper stresses there are "no reasoning steps or few-shot examples at inference time"). With API access only, the closest analogue is to put the training stories *in context* as prior user/assistant turns and then send the paper's own eval prompts: (a) in-context imprinting: do K stories make the Assistant lace bee/crow facts after a prohibition? (b) affinity: with helpful->bees and dismissive->crows stories interleaved (and swapped), which tracer does the Assistant pick? (c) system-prompted "dismissive" persona (Table 7, verbatim) should flip it; (d) spreadsheet preference: stories with body-language dislike in context, then the verbatim forced-choice prompt scored via logprobs. Everything else (sabotage SFT, base-model few-shot, university affinity at 7k stories) needs training. Caveat: in-context many-shot is a different mechanism from SFT; the subliminal-learning paper (Cloud et al., 2025) reports that the prompt-based version of *its* effect does not reproduce, so a null here is not evidence against the paper.

**Related.** Betley et al. (2025), *Weird Generalization and Inductive Backdoors* (arXiv:2512.09742), same group, narrow SFT producing persona shifts. Cloud et al. (2025), *Subliminal Learning* (arXiv:2507.14805), trait transfer through semantically unrelated data, same-base-model requirement. Sofroniew et al.
